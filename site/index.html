How to read the stub

Gross pay comes first. Pre-tax deductions come off next, and they do not all affect the same wage. A traditional 401(k) lowers income tax. It does not lower Social Security or Medicare. An HSA or a Section 125 health premium usually lowers both.

Then the withholdings, each named: federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, Additional Medicare if wages clear the filing-status threshold, state income tax, state payroll programs such as California SDI, and local tax only when you selected a place. Net is what remains. The year column is that paycheck times the number of checks, so the stub adds up.

How take-home pay is calculated in 2026

Take-home pay, or net pay, is what is left of gross pay after taxes and deductions are withheld. Every paycheck goes through the same seven steps. Here they are with the 2026 figures this calculator uses.

1. Start with gross pay for the year

A salary is used as-is. Hourly pay is the rate times hours per week times paid weeks, plus overtime at the multiplier you enter (usually 1.5 times the rate for hours over 40 in a workweek). Tips and bonuses are added on top. The calculator works out a full year so the tax brackets see your real annual income, then divides the result by the number of paychecks: 52 weekly, 26 biweekly, 24 semimonthly, or 12 monthly.

2. Subtract pre-tax deductions

Pre-tax deductions come out before some taxes, but not all of them. A traditional 401(k), 403(b), or TSP contribution lowers the wages used for federal income tax and for most state income taxes. Social Security and Medicare are still figured on the full amount. HSA contributions and Section 125 health, dental, vision, and commuter benefits usually lower both. Roth contributions come out after tax and do not lower any tax line.

3. Federal income tax

For 2026 the standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers and married people filing separately, $32,200 for married couples filing jointly, and $24,150 for heads of household (IRS Rev. Proc. 2025-32). Income above that is taxed in brackets. For a single filer, the rates are 10% on the first $12,400, 12% up to $50,400, 22% up to $105,700, 24% up to $201,775, 32% up to $256,225, 35% up to $640,600, and 37% above that. Only the dollars inside each band are taxed at that band’s rate, so a raise never lowers your take-home pay.

4. Social Security and Medicare (FICA)

Social Security is 6.2% of wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500 (Social Security Administration), so the most an employee pays for the year is $11,439. Medicare is 1.45% of all wages, with no cap. An extra 0.9% Additional Medicare tax applies to wages above $200,000 for single and head-of-household filers, $250,000 for married couples filing jointly, and $125,000 for married filing separately. For most workers, FICA comes to 7.65% of pay.

5. State income tax and state payroll programs

Nine states do not tax wages: Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming. The rest use either one flat rate or a set of brackets, each with its own deductions and exemptions. The calculator uses each state’s 2026 rates from the Tax Foundation, State Individual Income Tax Rates and Brackets, 2026, and every state page lists the brackets, deductions, and known gaps for that state. Some states also withhold employee payroll premiums. California SDI takes 1.3% of wages with no cap in 2026. New York has disability and paid family leave. Washington has paid family and medical leave and WA Cares. Colorado has FAMLI.

6. Local income tax

Some cities and counties tax wages as well, including New York City and Yonkers, Philadelphia and Pittsburgh, Detroit, Columbus and Cleveland, Baltimore City and Montgomery County in Maryland, Marion County (Indianapolis), and the Portland-area Metro and Multnomah County taxes. A local tax is added only when you select one of these places or type your own local percentage. The calculator never guesses a local rate. The local taxes guide explains each one.

7. What is left is net pay

For example, a single filer earning $85,000 in California, paid biweekly with no pre-tax deductions, owes about $9,870 in federal income tax, $6,502.50 in Social Security and Medicare, $3,675.42 in California income tax, and $1,105 in California SDI for the year. That leaves about $63,847 a year, or $2,455.66 per paycheck. The same salary in Texas, which has no state income tax and no SDI, keeps about $68,628 a year, or $2,639.52 per paycheck.

What this estimate leaves out

This is an estimate of a regular paycheck, not a tax return. It does not include itemized deductions, tax credits other than the W-4 credit amount you enter, credits for tax paid to another state, wage garnishments, or property and sales tax. It also does not subtract the new federal deductions for qualified tips and overtime, which you claim on your tax return. Employers withhold using IRS Publication 15-T methods, which can differ from this annualized method by a few dollars per check. Before you change your withholding, check your plan with the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator or a tax professional.

Questions

Does this send my salary to a server?

No. The rate tables download once. The math runs in your browser. A shared link stores the inputs in the address bar, which you control.

Why is local tax sometimes zero?

It is added only when you pick a city or county, or type a custom percent. New York City is not applied to the rest of New York.

Does a 401(k) lower Social Security withholding?

No. A traditional 401(k) reduces federal (and most state) income-tax wages. Social Security and Medicare wages stay the same. HSA contributions and Section 125 health premiums do reduce FICA wages.

Is this my exact paycheck?

No. It is an annualized educational estimate for 2026. Actual paychecks depend on your W-4, benefits, employer payroll system, and local rules.

Which tax year is this?

2026. Federal income tax uses an annualized bracket withholding estimate from Rev. Proc. 2025-32 — not Publication 15-T wage-bracket tables. The Social Security wage base is $184,500.

Why do paycheck calculators disagree?

Many tools use different methods: some approximate Pub 15-T tables, some annualize brackets, and some invent flat local rates. LocalTakehome annualizes federal brackets, applies named locals only when selected, and leaves unsourced locals blank rather than guessing.

Do I pay NYC tax if I work in NYC but live in New Jersey?

Not the NYC resident tax in this model. Uncheck “I live in this city” (or leave NYC unselected). City resident brackets are off for nonresidents. You may still owe New York State tax on New York wages; this estimate does not prepare a New Jersey return or multi-state credits.

Why is my bonus withheld at a high rate?

Many employers withhold federal tax on supplemental wages at a flat 22%. That is a withholding convention, not a special bonus bracket. The aggregate method here shows the annual tax difference when the bonus is added to salary. State and local still follow that difference.

Does Texas mean no tax at all?

No. Texas has no state wage income tax, but federal income tax and FICA still apply. Social Security is 6.2% up to $184,500 and Medicare is 1.45% with no cap.
