How much of a raise you actually keep

A raise is taxed at your marginal rate, which is the rate on your last dollars of income. It is not taxed at your average rate. That is why a 10% raise does not mean a 10% bigger deposit. The new dollars land in your highest bracket and pay Social Security, Medicare, and any state and local tax on top.

Example: $80,000 to $88,000 in Illinois

A single filer paid biweekly gets an $8,000 raise. Federal income tax rises by $1,760 (22% of the raise). Social Security and Medicare rise by $612 (7.65%). Illinois income tax rises by $396 (4.95%). Take-home pay goes up by $5,232 a year, or $201.24 per biweekly check. That is about $65.40 of every $100 of the raise.

Myth: a raise can push you into a higher bracket and lower your pay

That does not happen with federal income tax brackets. Only the dollars above a bracket threshold are taxed at the higher rate. Everything below is taxed the same as before. A raise always increases net pay under the federal brackets. A few benefit programs and credits have income cliffs, but those are not part of a paycheck.

Things that change how much you keep

  • The Social Security wage base. Once wages pass $184,500 in 2026, the 6.2% Social Security tax stops, so you keep more of each additional dollar for the rest of the year.
  • State brackets. In states with graduated brackets, a raise can also move your top dollars into a higher state rate. In states with no income tax, you keep more of the raise.
  • Pre-tax savings. If you contribute a percentage to a 401(k), part of the raise goes to savings automatically and lowers the income tax on the rest.

Questions

Can a raise make me take home less money?

Not because of federal income tax brackets. Only the income above each threshold is taxed at the higher rate, so net pay always rises when gross pay rises.

What is a marginal tax rate?

It is the combined rate on your next dollar of income: your federal bracket, plus Social Security and Medicare, plus your state and local rates. The raise calculator shows the share of each new $100 you keep after all of them.

Why is my raise smaller on the paycheck than I expected?

The raise is taxed at your highest rates. Percentage-based deductions, such as a 401(k) set as a percent of pay, also grow with your pay.
