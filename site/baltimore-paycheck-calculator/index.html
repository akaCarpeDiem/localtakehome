Maryland · True take-home pay for where you actually live

Baltimore City paycheck calculator

Baltimore City’s local income tax is 3.20% of Maryland taxable net income for 2026, the same published resident rate as Montgomery County, but it is a different jurisdiction on the stub. The city is treated like a county for Maryland local tax purposes. The state brackets from 2% through 6.5% still apply on the shared taxable base.

The personal exemption of $3,200 phases out as income rises, and the standard deduction is $3,350 single or $6,700 joint. A traditional 401(k) lowers both the state and the city lines together. Uncheck live-here for the simplified 2.25% nonresident treatment used in withholding facts; that is not a full nonresident return.

Montgomery County has its own page at the same 3.20% resident rate. Other counties need a custom percent. Do not assume every Maryland local rate is 3.20%; the Comptroller’s list still includes lower and higher figures elsewhere in the state.

Federal tax and FICA still dominate a typical Baltimore salary. Use the compare tool with Baltimore selected on one side and Maryland state-only on the other when an offer moves you across the city line.

Local tax

Baltimore City is modeled as a flat local tax: 3.2% for residents and 2.25% for nonresidents (when the rates differ). Use the live-here switch for resident versus nonresident treatment. Baltimore City's 2026 local rate is 3.20% of Maryland taxable net income. Nonresident switch uses the 2.25% simplification described on the Montgomery County record.

What is not included

Credits, itemized deductions, multi-state credits, property tax, sales tax, and unsourced local rates are not included. Employer payroll tables can differ from this annualized estimate. This Baltimore City page does not invent taxes that are missing from the 2026 data files.

Questions

Is Baltimore’s local rate the same as Montgomery County?

Both resident rates are 3.20% for 2026. Select the place that matches your residence.

Does Baltimore add a second city tax on top of a county tax?

No. Baltimore City’s local income tax replaces a county local tax for city residents.

Does a 401(k) reduce Baltimore local tax?

Yes in this model, because it lowers Maryland taxable income.
