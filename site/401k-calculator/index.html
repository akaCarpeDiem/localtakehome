How a traditional 401(k) changes your paycheck

A traditional 401(k) contribution comes out of your pay before federal income tax, and before state income tax in most states. That lowers your taxable wages, so your income tax goes down. Social Security and Medicare are still figured on your full pay. As a result, your paycheck shrinks by less than the amount you contribute.

2026 contribution limits

For 2026, employees can defer up to $24,500 into a 401(k), 403(b), governmental 457 plan, or the federal Thrift Savings Plan. Workers 50 and older can usually add an $8,000 catch-up contribution. Workers who are 60 to 63 in 2026 may be able to add up to $11,250 instead, if the plan allows it (IRS, 2026 retirement plan limits (Notice 2025-67)). This tool does not add catch-up contributions automatically. Enter them as a dollar amount on the main calculator.

Exceptions worth knowing

  • Pennsylvania does not exclude employee 401(k) contributions from state taxable wages, so the state line does not fall. New Jersey also taxes them at the state level.
  • Philadelphia and Detroit local wage taxes are figured on gross wages, so a 401(k) does not lower them.
  • Roth 401(k) contributions are taken out after tax. They lower your paycheck dollar for dollar today, and qualified withdrawals later are tax-free.
  • Employer matching contributions are not part of your paycheck and do not change take-home pay.

Questions

Does a 401(k) lower Social Security and Medicare taxes?

No. Traditional 401(k) deferrals reduce wages for income tax only. Social Security and Medicare are still withheld on the full amount.

How much will my paycheck drop if I contribute 5%?

Less than 5% of your gross pay, because your income tax also falls. The table on this page shows the change at 0%, 3%, 5%, 6%, 10%, and 15% for the salary and state you enter.

What is the 401(k) limit for 2026?

$24,500 in employee deferrals. People 50 and older can usually add $8,000 more, and people 60 to 63 may be able to add up to $11,250 if their plan allows it.
