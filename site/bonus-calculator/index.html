Aggregate versus 22%

The aggregate method adds the bonus to the year and subtracts the tax on salary alone. That is the difference in the annual bill under this estimate. Many employers instead withhold federal tax on supplemental wages at a flat 22%. That is a withholding convention, not a special tax bracket. If you are already in the 22% federal bracket, the two federal numbers often match. State and local tax still follow the aggregate difference here.

Questions

Is a bonus taxed at 40%?

Not as a special rate. Withholding can look heavy because it is a lump sum on top of wages that have already used the standard deduction. The return uses the same brackets as the rest of your income.

What is the aggregate method?

Add the bonus to annual wages, compute tax, then subtract the tax on salary alone. That difference is the tax attributable to the bonus under this estimate.

What is the 22% supplemental rate?

Many employers withhold federal tax on supplemental wages at a flat 22%. That is a withholding convention, not a different federal bracket. State and local here still follow the aggregate difference.

Example: a $5,000 bonus on a $70,000 salary in New York

A single filer paid biweekly, with no city selected, gets a $5,000 bonus. Under the aggregate method, the bonus adds $1,100 of federal income tax (22%), $310 of Social Security, $72.50 of Medicare, $270 of New York State income tax, and $21.60 of New York payroll premiums. About $3,225.90 of the bonus is left. A flat 22% supplemental withholding would also withhold $1,100 of federal tax, so both methods match at this income.

When the two methods differ

If your salary puts your top dollars in the 10% or 12% bracket, a flat 22% withholds more federal tax than the bonus will actually cost you, and the difference comes back as a refund. If your top dollars are taxed at 24% or more, a flat 22% withholds too little, and you may owe the difference when you file. Supplemental wages over $1 million in a year must be withheld at 37% (IRS Publication 15 (Circular E)). Social Security, Medicare, and state tax apply to a bonus the same way they apply to regular pay.
