How hourly pay turns into take-home pay

An hourly paycheck starts as rate times hours. To estimate tax correctly, the calculator first turns that into a yearly figure: hourly rate × hours per week × paid weeks. At $25 an hour, 40 hours a week, and 52 weeks, that is $52,000 a year. Tax brackets, the standard deduction, and state brackets are all set for a full year. Working out the year first and then dividing it into paychecks gives a much better estimate than taxing one week by itself.

Overtime, shift differentials, and mixed rates

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, most non-exempt employees must be paid at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek (U.S. Department of Labor, overtime pay). Enter your weekly overtime hours and the multiplier, and the calculator adds them to the year. If one paycheck mixes several rates, such as base hours, a night or weekend differential, hazard pay, or overtime on top of a differential, choose Add rate blocks under the hourly fields. Each block is a label, a number of hours, and an hourly rate. You can estimate a single pay period (“This pay period”) or project a full year.

Part-time hours and unpaid weeks

If you are paid for fewer than 52 weeks because of seasonal work, unpaid leave, or a school-year schedule, lower the paid weeks. Less annual income can put part of your pay in a lower bracket, so the take-home pay per check can change by more than you expect.

Tips

Reported tips count as wages for income tax and for Social Security and Medicare. The calculator adds the yearly tips you enter to gross pay. For 2025 through 2028, federal law also lets eligible workers deduct up to $25,000 of qualified tips on their tax return. That deduction shrinks once modified adjusted gross income passes $150,000 ($300,000 for joint filers). You claim it on your return, or you can reflect it on Form W-4 Step 4(b). It is not subtracted from this paycheck estimate, and tips still owe Social Security and Medicare.

Questions

How do I convert an hourly wage to a yearly salary?

Multiply the hourly rate by the hours you work each week, then by the weeks you are paid. At 40 hours and 52 weeks, that is 2,080 hours, so $20 an hour is $41,600 a year before tax.

How much is $20 an hour after taxes?

For a single filer in Florida working 40 hours a week for 52 weeks, this estimate comes to about $35,606 a year after federal income tax, Social Security, and Medicare, or about $1,369.45 per biweekly paycheck. States with an income tax will be lower.

Are hourly workers taxed differently from salaried workers?

No. Hourly and salaried pay are both wages and use the same federal brackets, FICA rates, and state rules. The only difference is how gross pay is figured.

Is overtime taxed at a higher rate?

No. Overtime is regular wages. It is added to your other pay and taxed at your normal rates. A large overtime check can look heavily taxed because payroll treats that one check as if every check were that large.
