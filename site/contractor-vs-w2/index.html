Contractor vs W-2

How contractor (1099) income is taxed compared with W-2 pay

A W-2 employee splits Social Security and Medicare with the employer, and each side pays 7.65%. An independent contractor pays both halves as self-employment tax: 12.4% for Social Security up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, plus 2.9% for Medicare on all net earnings, for 15.3% in total. Self-employment tax is figured on 92.35% of net profit, which is income minus business expenses. Half of it is then deducted before income tax (IRS, Self-employment tax).

Example: $100,000 in Texas with $8,000 of expenses

As a W-2 employee, a single filer keeps about $79,180 a year after federal income tax and FICA. As a contractor with the same $100,000 in gross receipts and $8,000 of business expenses, self-employment tax is about $12,999. That is 15.3% of 92.35% of the $92,000 profit. Federal income tax is lower, about $9,980, because of the expenses and the half-of-SE-tax deduction. Net income after expenses and tax is about $69,021. That is roughly $10,000 less than the W-2 offer, and it comes before the contractor pays for health insurance or retirement savings on their own.

Quarterly estimated taxes

Nobody withholds tax from contractor pay, so the IRS expects quarterly estimated payments. For most people they are due around April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15 of the next year (IRS, Estimated taxes). The tool shows a rough quarterly set-aside, which is the estimated yearly tax divided by four. It is not a Form 1040-ES voucher.

What this comparison leaves out

The qualified business income deduction is not included, so a contractor’s income tax may be overstated. Health insurance, retirement contributions, state disability coverage, and business costs beyond the expense figure you enter are not included either. When you compare a contractor rate with a salary, remember that a W-2 job may also include paid time off, an employer retirement match, and part of your health premiums.

Questions

What is the self-employment tax rate for 2026?

15.3% of 92.35% of net self-employment earnings: 12.4% Social Security on earnings up to $184,500 and 2.9% Medicare on all of it. A 0.9% Additional Medicare tax applies above the filing-status threshold.

How much more should a contractor charge than a W-2 salary?

It depends on benefits and expenses. The contractor pays the employer’s half of FICA, about 7.65%, plus their own insurance, retirement savings, unpaid time off, and business costs. This tool shows the tax difference only.

Do contractors have to pay quarterly taxes?

Usually yes, if they expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year. Payments are generally due in April, June, September, and January.
