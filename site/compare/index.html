What the difference means

The table is an annualized estimate, then split into biweekly checks. A higher net is not a full cost-of-living comparison. Rent, commuting, and sales tax are outside the stub. Reciprocity between two states is not computed; each column taxes only the state you chose for it.

Comparing two job offers or two places

Two offers with the same salary can lead to very different deposits. Federal income tax and FICA are the same everywhere in the U.S., so the gap comes from state income tax, state payroll programs, and local wage taxes. This tool runs both offers through the same 2026 engine with the same filing status and pay frequency, so the only differences are the ones you change.

Example: $85,000 in Texas or California

A single filer paid biweekly with no deductions keeps about $68,627.50 a year in Texas, or $2,639.52 per check. In California, the same salary keeps about $63,847.08, or $2,455.66 per check. The $4,780.42 yearly difference is California income tax ($3,675.42) plus California SDI ($1,105). The federal tax and FICA lines are identical.

What a fair comparison should also include

  • Cost of living. Housing, commuting, insurance, and sales and property taxes often matter more than the tax difference. None of these appear on a paycheck.
  • Living in one state and working in another. Usually your home state taxes all your income and gives a credit for tax paid to the state where you work. Some neighboring states have reciprocity agreements, so only your home state withholds. This tool taxes each offer only in the state you pick for it.
  • Local taxes. A city wage tax, such as New York City, Philadelphia, or Detroit, can be the biggest single difference between two offers in the same state. Pick the city on the side where it applies.
  • Benefits. Employer health premiums, retirement matching, and paid leave differ between offers, and they can be worth thousands of dollars a year.

Questions

Why is federal tax the same in both columns?

Federal income tax, Social Security, and Medicare do not depend on where you live. Only state, state payroll, and local lines change when you move.

Which states have no income tax on wages?

Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming do not tax wages. Washington does have payroll premiums for paid leave and long-term care.

Does this include cost of living?

No. It compares taxes withheld from pay. Rent, home prices, commuting, and sales and property taxes are outside the paycheck and are not included.
