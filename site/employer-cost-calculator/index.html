What employers pay on top of salary

A salary is not the whole cost of an employee. The employer also pays its own share of payroll taxes and whatever benefits it offers. This tool adds up the parts that can be stated with confidence.

  • Social Security (employer share): 6.2% of wages up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500. That is the same amount withheld from the employee.
  • Medicare (employer share): 1.45% of all wages. The 0.9% Additional Medicare tax on high earners is paid by the employee only. Employers do not match it.
  • FUTA: The federal unemployment tax is 6.0% of the first $7,000 of each employee’s wages. Employers that pay state unemployment tax on time usually get a credit of up to 5.4%, which brings the net rate to 0.6%, or $42 per employee per year. Employers in a “credit reduction” state pay more (IRS, Federal unemployment (FUTA) tax).
  • State unemployment (SUTA): The state sets this rate for each employer, based on its history, and it applies only up to a state wage base. The tool leaves it at zero until you enter your rate.
  • State paid-leave programs: Some states charge employers part of a paid family or medical leave premium. One example is Colorado FAMLI, which is included when you choose Colorado.
  • Benefits: Employer retirement contributions and health insurance premiums are added as dollar amounts you enter.

Example: $75,000 employee in Colorado

With a $3,000 employer retirement contribution and $6,000 toward health insurance, the employer pays $4,650 in Social Security, $1,087.50 in Medicare, $42 in FUTA, and $330 for the employer share of Colorado FAMLI. Adding the benefits brings the total to about $90,109.50, which is roughly 20% above the salary, before any state unemployment tax.

What is not included

Workers’ compensation insurance, local employer taxes, payroll software or service fees, equipment, office space, training, and recruiting are not included. Paid time off is already part of the salary, so it is not counted twice. For employer filing rules, see IRS Publication 15 (Circular E).

Questions

How much does an employee cost on top of salary?

At least 7.65% for the employer share of Social Security and Medicare, plus FUTA, state unemployment, and any benefits. In the Colorado example on this page, payroll taxes and $9,000 of benefits bring a $75,000 salary to about $90,110, roughly 20% more.

Does the employer pay the Additional Medicare tax?

No. The 0.9% Additional Medicare tax on wages above $200,000 is withheld from the employee only. The employer does not match it.

Why is state unemployment blank?

Each state sets an experience-based rate for each employer, often with a separate rate for new employers. A made-up rate would be misleading, so the tool waits for you to enter yours.
