Maryland

Maryland paycheck calculator

Maryland’s state brackets are only half the income tax. Every county, and Baltimore City, adds a local rate on Maryland taxable net income. The state piece runs from 2% up through new high-income brackets of 6.25% and 6.5%. The local piece for Montgomery County and Baltimore City is 3.20% in the Comptroller’s 2026 withholding facts. On a typical salary the county line can rival the state line. Leaving it off is how other calculators flatter a Maryland offer.

The personal exemption is $3,200 and phases out as income climbs. This estimate fades it in a straight line between $100,000 and $150,000 for a single filer, and between $150,000 and $200,000 for a joint return. The standard deduction is small, $3,350 single and $6,700 joint. A traditional 401(k) reduces the shared base, so both the state and the county fall together. FICA does not.

Other counties are a custom percent. The Comptroller publishes the full list, including places at 2.25% and at least one at 3.30%. Nonresidents are a simplification here: the switch uses 2.25%, which is a withholding convention, not a promise about your county. Reciprocity with D.C., Virginia, West Virginia, and Pennsylvania is noted when you mark a work state. It is not a second return.

Maryland income tax rules used for 2026

Maryland state rates run from 2% to 6.5% after a small standard deduction and a $3,200 exemption. Every county adds a local rate on the same taxable income. Most counties plus Baltimore City are named in the calculator’s local-tax list (Anne Arundel tiered; Frederick County cliff schedule uses the custom percent).

Local tax

Every Maryland county and Baltimore City adds a local rate on Maryland taxable income. Montgomery County and Baltimore City are named at 3.20%. Other counties belong in the custom percent field.

What is not included

Credits, itemized deductions, multi-state credits, property tax, sales tax, and unsourced local rates are not included. Employer payroll tables can differ from this annualized estimate. Maryland counties other than the named ones must be entered as a custom percent.

Questions

Do I have to pick a Maryland county?

If you want a realistic net, yes. The state tax alone understates a Maryland paycheck. Montgomery County and Baltimore City are named at 3.20%. Use a custom percent for other counties.

Is the local tax a percent of the state tax?

No. It is a percent of Maryland taxable income, the same base the state brackets use.

Does a 401(k) reduce the county tax?

Yes in this model. The deferral lowers taxable income, and both the state and the county are computed on that income.
