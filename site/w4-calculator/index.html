W-4

W-4 helper

The 2020 W-4 does not ask for allowances. It asks for a dollar credit, other income, other deductions, extra withholding, and a checkbox if you have more than one job. This page shows how those entries move an estimate. It does not fill a legal form.

Step 2, the multiple-jobs checkbox, is approximated here with half the standard deduction and narrower brackets. That increases withholding. It is not the IRS worksheet that uses the other job’s wages.

Step 3 is a dollar credit for the year, not a count of people. Step 4(a) adds other income. Step 4(b) adds deductions beyond the standard deduction. Step 4(c) is extra withholding on every check, and it does not change the bracket math.

How each step of Form W-4 changes your paycheck

  • Step 1, filing status: sets the standard deduction and bracket widths used for withholding. In 2026 the standard deduction is $16,100 single, $32,200 married filing jointly, and $24,150 head of household.
  • Step 2, multiple jobs or a working spouse: when checked, each job withholds as if it gets only half the standard deduction and half-width brackets. This raises withholding so two incomes are not each taxed as if they were the only one.
  • Step 3, dependents and other credits: a dollar amount for the year, such as $2,200 for each qualifying child under 17. It lowers federal withholding by the same amount spread over the year’s paychecks.
  • Step 4(a), other income: income without withholding, such as interest or a side job, so tax on it is collected through your paycheck.
  • Step 4(b), deductions: expected deductions beyond the standard deduction. For 2025 through 2028, the IRS worksheet for this step also covers the new deductions for qualified tips, qualified overtime, car loan interest, and the additional deduction for people 65 and older.
  • Step 4(c), extra withholding: a flat dollar amount withheld from every check. It does not change the bracket calculation.

When to update your W-4

Review your W-4 when you start a new job, get married or divorced, have a child, add a second job, start earning overtime or tips, or get a large refund or tax bill. Your employer uses the newest W-4 you give them. A state may have its own withholding form, and changing your federal W-4 does not always change state withholding.

The W-4 has not used allowances since 2020, so answers like “claim 0 or 1” no longer apply. For a full check of your year, including other income and credits, use the IRS Tax Withholding Estimator. The form and its instructions are at IRS, About Form W-4.

Questions

Should I claim 0 or 1 on my W-4?

The current W-4 has no allowances. Instead, you check a box for multiple jobs, enter a dollar amount of credits, list other income or deductions, and can add extra withholding per check.

Why did checking Step 2 lower my paycheck?

Step 2 tells payroll that you have another job or a working spouse. Each job then withholds as if it gets only half of the standard deduction and narrower brackets, which raises withholding.

Does the W-4 change my actual tax?

No. It changes how much is withheld during the year. Your actual tax is figured on your return. Too little withholding means a balance due, and too much means a refund.
