Arizona

Arizona paycheck calculator

Arizona taxes wages at 2.5%. The awkward part is the deduction. The Tax Foundation’s 2026 table shows $8,350 single and $16,700 joint, because some states that point at the federal standard deduction are still tied to a pre-OBBBA version of the code. If Arizona has since conformed to the higher federal amounts, $16,100 and $32,200, this state line runs high. The page says so on the stub rather than quietly picking the friendlier number.

There is no personal exemption in the table, and dependent relief is a small credit, applied when you enter a count. Phoenix, Tucson, and the other large cities do not add a wage income tax here. Federal tax and FICA remain the main withholdings on a typical salary.

Use the result as a planning range. A 2.5% state is hard to get wildly wrong; a doubled deduction changes the bill by a couple hundred dollars, not by a different kind of tax. The methodology lists this conformity gap with the other estimated pieces.

Arizona income tax rules used for 2026

Arizona taxes wages at a flat 2.5%. The deduction used here is $8,350 single / $16,700 joint, the pre-OBBBA federal standard deduction some conformity states still point at.

Rate: a flat 2.5% on taxable income for every filing status.

  • Standard deduction: $8,350 single, $16,700 married filing jointly.
  • Personal credit: $100 per dependent (subtracted from tax).
  • Traditional 401(k) contributions reduce Arizona taxable wages in this model.

Details and simplifications: Tax Foundation marks Arizona as conforming to a pre-OBBBA federal standard deduction ($8,350 / $16,700 for 2026). A post-OBBBA conformity bill would raise that deduction. Dependent relief is a small credit, modeled at $100 per dependent.

Why this line is an estimate: If Arizona updates conformity to the higher federal standard deduction, this state line is too high.

Confidence: an estimate (part of the state rule is simplified, as noted above). Source: Tax Foundation, State Individual Income Tax Rates and Brackets, 2026. Last verified September 27, 2026.

Local tax

Arizona does not have a local wage tax in this file. City costs show up in rent and sales tax, not as a second income-tax line on the stub.

What is not included

Credits, itemized deductions, multi-state credits, property tax, sales tax, and unsourced local rates are not included. Employer payroll tables can differ from this annualized estimate. This Arizona page does not invent taxes that are missing from the 2026 data files.

Questions

Is Arizona a flat tax?

Yes. 2.5% of taxable wages in this model.

Why might the Arizona line be too high?

The standard deduction used here is the smaller pre-OBBBA conformity figure. A law adopting the higher federal deduction would lower the tax.

Does Phoenix tax wages?

Not in this calculator. Add a custom percent only if you have a real local wage tax.
